# Educational content references

Reviewed 12 October 2026. These support the training narrative; they are not a building-code compliance certification or a substitute for an instructor's review.

- U.S. Fire Administration, Choosing and Using Fire Extinguishers: https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/fire-extinguishers/index.html
  - Assess a small, contained fire, correct extinguisher, ability to operate it and a clear escape route before attempting suppression.
  - PASS: pull the pin, aim low at the base, squeeze the handle, sweep side to side.
  - Successful safe evacuation must remain a valid outcome when suppression is unsafe. Do not penalize the learner for leaving an unsafe fire.
- Pennsylvania Office of the State Fire Commissioner, Fire Sprinkler Safety: https://www.pa.gov/agencies/osfc/communities/home-sprinklers
  - Conventional heads activate separately through their heat-sensitive element. Smoke detection must not automatically activate every sprinkler.

In-game numeric heat/smoke limits and propagation speeds will be simplified illustrative parameters. Do not present them as measured real-world survival or equipment activation limits. Provide an instructor-facing explanation of these simplifications.

## Phase 5 evacuation references — verified 17 October 2026

- U.S. Fire Administration, Escape planning pictographs: https://www.usfa.fema.gov/gallery/pictographs/escape-planning.html
- U.S. Fire Administration, Home fire escape plans: https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/home-fire-escape-plans/
- U.S. Fire Administration, Hotel and motel fire safety: https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/hotel-fires/index.html

Applied principles: identify alternative routes, avoid smoke, leave promptly, meet at an outside assembly point, stay outside and report to the person in charge. These general references inform the demonstration; the fictional classroom layout is not a certified evacuation plan for a real college building. Its two classroom routes share a passage and courtyard exits.

The route smoke index uses the existing accumulated simulation smoke multiplied by illustrative transport coefficients (front 1.35, rear 0.45). A route is excluded at index 0.16 or local simulated heat 65, or when a physical obstruction is detected. These coefficients and thresholds are gameplay parameters, not measured smoke concentration, validated ventilation modelling or real-world safe-exposure limits. The HUD explicitly labels smoke as a relative simulation index. Assembly checks local heat below the illustrative value 40. Certified training and the institution's actual emergency plan remain authoritative.
